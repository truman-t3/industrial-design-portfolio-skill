## Evidence levels

| Level | Meaning | Examples | Presentation rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| E0 | Unsupported idea | AI suggestion, memory, intuition | Label `Assumption` |
| E1 | Supplied but unverified material | User note, old slide, unattributed number | Cite source and caveat |
| E2 | Direct project evidence | Photo, sketch, CAD screenshot, test note, dated prototype | Present as project evidence |
| E3 | Independently verifiable result | Published spec, supplier quote, test report, launched product metric | Cite origin and date |

Visual polish never raises an evidence level.

## Claim rules

- Research claims require participants, method, date/context, and limitations when available.
- Ergonomic claims require target population, dimensions or test method, and use posture.
- Performance claims require a benchmark, measurement method, and unit.
- Cost claims require quantity, currency, date, included/excluded costs, and source.
- Manufacturing claims require process assumptions, material, expected volume, and review status.
- Patent or freedom-to-operate claims require a named database/search date and professional caveat.
- Sustainability claims require a boundary, comparison basis, or declared estimate method.
- Impact claims require a before/after definition and source.

If these fields are unavailable, rewrite as design intent or a validation plan.

## Discipline-specific proof

### Research and strategy

Strong: observation records, interview excerpts with consent, journey evidence, competitive teardown, prioritized requirements.

Weak: generic personas, uncited market numbers, post-rationalized pain points.

### Form and CMF

Strong: sketch families, proportion studies, surface evolution, CMF samples, rationale tied to use and brand.

Weak: one photorealistic render with no alternatives or material logic.

### Ergonomics and interaction

Strong: hand/body relationship, reach/clearance diagrams, physical rigs, error states, accessibility considerations.

Weak: declaring a product ergonomic from appearance alone.

### Engineering and architecture

Strong: CAD screenshots, component architecture, interfaces, assembly sequence, service paths, engineer review notes.

Weak: generated exploded art presented as a technical drawing.

### Manufacturing and viability

Strong: process choice, draft angles, wall thickness intent, parting strategy, fastening, tolerance risks, supplier feedback.

Weak: unexplained process labels or precise costs without sources.

### Prototyping and testing

Strong: prototype purpose, variable tested, method, observation, design change.

Weak: model photos without explaining what was learned.

## Authorship

Every project must state:

- personal role;
- team and collaborators;
- work personally produced;
- inherited constraints or assets;
- tools used, including generative AI where material.

Use verbs that reflect actual responsibility: `led`, `co-designed`, `modeled`, `visualized`, `tested`, `supported`, or `documented`.

## Common credibility failures

- fabricated research participants or quotes;
- AI images mixed with prototype photos without disclosure;
- precise dimensions that do not match across views;
- unattributed competitor imagery;
- supplier or patent conclusions without a search trail;
- team outcomes written as sole authorship;
- final render shown before the reviewer understands the decision;
- too many process diagrams and too little real process evidence.
