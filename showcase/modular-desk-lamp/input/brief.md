# Modular Desk Lamp — Fictional Input Brief

> FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION INPUT. This brief was created to exercise the Skill. It contains no real client, user, company, research, prototype, test, supplier, cost, or launch data.

## Requested deliverable

Create a 14-page horizontal industrial design case study for designers evaluating the `industrial-design-portfolio` Skill. The portfolio should work in a browser, at mobile width, and when printed to PDF.

## Concept proposition

Explore a repairable desk-light platform for compact workspaces. The concept may shift between focused task lighting, ambient lighting, and video-call lighting by reconfiguring its light head, arm, and base.

This is a proposition to investigate, not a user need that has been observed or validated.

## Intended product language

- circular weighted base;
- two slim arm segments;
- cylindrical joints;
- capsule-shaped light head;
- deep-blue matte aluminum;
- warm-white diffuser;
- cobalt replaceable sleeve.

These attributes describe visual intent only. They do not prove dimensions, materials, manufacturability, optical performance, stability, thermal performance, repairability, or cost.

## Intended story

1. State the proposition and its limits.
2. Show three synthetic usage scenarios as hypotheses.
3. Compare clamp, weighted-base, and rail-mounted concept routes.
4. Select the weighted-base route provisionally.
5. Define CMF and conceptual product architecture.
6. Describe a future validation plan.
7. Show an illustrative concept revision.
8. End with the unresolved questions.

## Non-negotiable disclosure rules

- Mark every behavioral statement as a hypothesis or synthetic scenario.
- Mark every generated product image as AI-assisted concept visualization.
- Call the exploded view a conceptual architecture illustration, not an engineering drawing.
- Describe validation activities as future plans, never completed tests.
- Do not invent people, quotes, measurements, dates, costs, or performance results.
- End the case study with unresolved gaps rather than implied readiness.
